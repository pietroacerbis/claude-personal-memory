# Voice, outreach

## Base rules

Tested on months of cold emails and LinkedIn messages. The sections below fill with my own rules, from my sent emails and my corrections; a rule of mine wins over a base rule.

- **Write as a peer who does the same job**, never as a vendor selling into it.
- **Dry over warm.** At send time, cut clauses: the plainer sentence stays.
- **Real numbers and real names** beat a worked example.
- **An observation is about what happens at this stage to anybody**, never a diagnosis of the reader.
- **Open from their own words**: something they published, applied to their own work.
- **With a senior expert, concede first**: say why pitching them would be absurd, then say the thing.
- **Name the product once**, on the first message; after that, refer to it plainly.
- **What is not shipped yet is written as "imagine if you could"**, never as done.
- **A give-first offer shows the criteria, never the names.**
- **Close on an offer**, like "if it interests you, I'll send two lines", rather than a question.
- **Never**: anything the reader can hear as an accusation, a word-for-word translation, anything inaccurate however small.
