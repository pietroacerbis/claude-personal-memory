# Voice, content

## Base rules

Tested on months of LinkedIn posts. The sections below fill with my own rules, from my posts and my corrections; a rule of mine wins over a base rule.

- **The first line is a real, specific moment**, or a small confession, never a boast. It lands before "see more", in about 210 characters.
- **A rhetorical question never opens a post.** It can carry the reader across the middle, once.
- **One idea per post.** A second idea is a second post.
- **Every claim has a concrete anchor**: a real number, a named tool, a real objection.
- **A sentence that explains, then a short one on its own line**, at least twice a post.
- **Lists as two-line bullets**: a header line, then one line of detail beneath.
- **One or two asides in brackets**, a notch warmer than the text around them.
- **Cut the second thing that says the first thing**, and any framework with a name.
- **The close is a question about the reader's own situation, or one concrete line**, never a grand statement.
- **Every post stands alone**: no "tomorrow I'll tell you why".
- **Never**: `not X, but Y`, `most people`, the long dash, three beats in a row like `Build. Test. Scale.`, `excited`, `thrilled`, `humbled`, `journey`, `game-changer`, `leverage`, `unlock`, `empower`, `supercharge`, an exclamation mark in an analytical post.
